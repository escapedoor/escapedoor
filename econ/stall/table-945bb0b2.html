SOLD OUT

What you now know
  1. Demand is how many people will buy at each price: the higher the price, the fewer they buy. Changing the price moves you along the demand curve.
  2. Other things (the weather, a rival, a rumour) move the whole curve. Never mix points from two different days.
  3. Where supply and demand meet, everything made is sold and nobody waits. A price held below that point makes a shortage: a queue.
  4. Fixed costs stay the same however much you sell; variable costs go up with every cup. Cost one cup at today’s prices.
  5. Profit = revenue − cost. Selling more is not the same as earning more, and a free cup still costs money.
  6. The opportunity cost of a choice is the best thing you give up: one thing, not all of them. Time and swaps have costs too.
  7. People respond to incentives. A prize for selling the most things rewarded giving lemonade away, and a “fair price” made people pay in time instead of money.
