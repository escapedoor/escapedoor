NOT GUILTY

What you now know
  1. In a criminal trial the prosecution must prove the case and make the jury sure. The defendant does not have to prove anything, not even where he was.
  2. “Not guilty” means that the prosecution did not make the jury sure. It does not mean “innocent”.
  3. An honest witness who is sure can still be wrong. Test what she says against facts: how far away, what light, how long, what was in the way, and whether she saw a face or a back.
  4. What a witness said at the time is worth more than what she remembers months later. One photograph, shown at the wrong moment, can take the place of a memory.
  5. An alibi is strong when a stranger who gains nothing, or a record made at the time, covers the very minute. A weak alibi is not a false one.
  6. A witness tells the jury what she saw or heard herself. What somebody else told her is hearsay, and the judge decides whether the jury may use it.
  7. Twelve jurors decide the facts. The judge directs them on the law, and answers their questions from the written directions and the exhibits, without telling them what to decide.
