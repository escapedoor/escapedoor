CLAIM DISMISSED

What you now know
  1. A civil court decides what one person owes another. The claimant asks, the defendant is asked, and nobody is “guilty”.
  2. The claimant has the burden of proof: whoever makes the claim has to prove it. The other side does not have to prove anything.
  3. The standard is the balance of probabilities: more likely than not. If the scales hang level, the claim fails.
  4. To find the issue, cross out everything that both sides agree about. What is left is what the judge must decide.
  5. MUST HAVE and CANNOT HAVE are for what the evidence proves. MIGHT HAVE is for what only fits. A judgment has no “might have” in it.
  6. In cross-examination you do not argue. You press for detail, and you put a paper, or the witness’s own words, beside what he has said.
  7. An honest witness can be quite sure and quite wrong. And a statement can be true in every word without being the whole truth.
