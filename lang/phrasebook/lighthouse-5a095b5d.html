Understood

What you now know
  1. You can work out a language from examples: compare sentences that differ in one place, find the piece that changed, and test it on a new sentence.
  2. Languages put words in different orders. English is subject-verb-object; Isuvo, Japanese and Korean put the verb last. Most of the world’s languages are SOV or SVO.
  3. An ending or a particle can show who did what to whom. Then the words can move and the meaning stays the same.
  4. Plurals differ: English says “two eggs”, but Isuvo and Turkish drop the plural after a number, and in Mandarin Chinese most nouns never change at all.
  5. Many languages, like Japanese, Korean and Isuvo, build politeness into the verb. English uses extra words, such as “please”.
  6. Words are made of morphemes: roots, prefixes and suffixes (un-break-able); a compound joins two roots (light-house). Not every bit that looks like a piece is one (carpet).
  7. A false friend looks like a word you know and means something else. On Isu, a pea is an egg.
