What you now know
  1. Acids, such as lemon juice, vinegar and cream of tartar, have a pH below 7. Alkalis, such as bicarbonate of soda, soap and washing soda, have a pH above 7. Pure water is neutral: 7.
  2. An indicator is a dye that changes colour with pH. Litmus only takes sides: red in an acid, blue in an alkali. Universal indicator gives a number.
  3. Every indicator has colours of its own. Red cabbage is red or pink in an acid and purple when neutral; then blue, green and yellow as the alkali gets stronger.
  4. An acid and an alkali cancel each other. That is neutralising, and it is how the cake was mended.
  5. A hot thing cools quickly at first and then more and more slowly, because the speed depends on how much hotter it is than the room. On a graph that is a curve. A straight line gives the wrong time.
  6. Pattern and size only say what kind of shoe it was. Wear and damage pick out the one shoe. Where two marks cross, the later one lies on top. A foot is about 15% of a person’s height: a rough guide.
  7. A pen leaves dents in the sheet underneath. A light held low at one side fills them with shadow, and the writing can be read.
