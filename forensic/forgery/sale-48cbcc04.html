What you now know
  1. Most white paper made since about 1950 glows blue-white under UV light, because of optical brighteners. Old paper stays dull.
  2. A glow tells you about the sheet, not about the words on it. Some new paper has no brighteners, and washing powder can make an old thing glow.
  3. Density = mass ÷ volume. The volume of an odd shape is the water it pushes up in a measuring cylinder. Gold is 19.3 g/cm³; the “gold” bird was 8.5: gilded brass.
  4. A pigment cannot be older than its invention: Prussian blue about 1704, titanium white the 1920s. It dates its own layer, not the whole picture.
  5. In a cross-section the oldest layer is at the bottom. Paint on top of the old varnish was added after the picture was finished, by a restorer or by somebody less honest.
  6. An oak adds one ring a year. Matching the pattern to a reference chart dates every ring, but the last ring is not the felling year: add the missing sapwood, at least nine rings in Baltic oak.
  7. Old materials do not make an old object. Every layer of a thing has its own date, and one impossible detail is enough to give a forgery away.
