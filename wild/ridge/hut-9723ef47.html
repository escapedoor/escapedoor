What you now know
  1. A grid reference gives eastings first and then northings: along the corridor, then up the stairs. Six figures name a square 100 metres across.
  2. At a scale of 1:25 000, 4 cm on the map is 1 km on the ground.
  3. A contour joins places of the same height. Lines close together mean steep ground; far apart, gentle. V’s that point uphill are a valley, and V’s that point downhill are a spur.
  4. A bearing is a direction in degrees, clockwise from north. On the map: edge from here to there, red lines along the grid, read the number. On the ground: red in the shed, and walk where the arrow points.
  5. A back bearing is the way back: 180 degrees different.
  6. Naismith’s rule: an hour for every 5 km, and an hour more for every 600 m climbed. Then allow for your own party.
  7. The distress signal is six blasts or flashes in a minute, a minute of silence, and six again. The answer is three. And when you send a message, say where you are going as well as where you are.
