What you now know
  1. A machine that answers only what it is asked needs the right kind of question: a yes-no question gets yes or no and nothing more, and “Is there…?” is not “How many…?”.
  2. An answer can be true and still mislead. “Can you see the shield?” “No.” did not mean that the shield had gone.
  3. A question that takes something for granted that is not true gets back only the mistake.
  4. Tenses give the order of events: the past perfect looks back from a later moment, and “nothing has happened” can only be written before something does.
  5. A log can be lying (the writer knows it is untrue), mistaken (the writer believes something she worked out wrongly) or incomplete (true, with something that matters left out).
  6. In space a ship coasts at the same speed in the same direction until something pushes it. Turning it round does not turn its journey round: it is the first step of braking.
  7. A test needs a control. A dark picture means something only if the same camera can see a star when nothing is in the way.

Say it aloud: explain to a friend, in four or five sentences, why turning a spaceship round does not send it home.
