IN TIME
Nepal → London · 30 May to 2 June 1953

What you now know
  1. A cipher changes letters, and gibberish tells everybody that there is a secret. A code changes meanings: a whole phrase stands for a whole message, and it means what the code book says, whatever it sounds like.
  2. The Times’s code made the best news sound like bad news, so that anybody who overheard it would yawn and pass it on. One word changed, and a phrase means nothing.
  3. Air gets thinner as you climb. At Base Camp a breath holds about half the oxygen it holds at sea level, on the summit about a third. The body gets used to it slowly, so climbers went up and down; the cure for mountain sickness is to go down.
  4. Time is distance divided by speed, and climbing costs extra time. A short path that climbs can be slower than a long one that does not. An honest estimate says “about”.
  5. In 1953 India and Nepal were 5 hours 30 minutes ahead of Greenwich. London in summer is 1 hour ahead of Greenwich, so Nepal was 4 hours 30 minutes ahead of London: a day ends in Kathmandu while London still has the evening.
  6. A report is no newer than its news. Find the day the news set out before you believe it.
  7. To meet a deadline, work back from it: take away the time each step needs, and you know the latest moment for the step before.
