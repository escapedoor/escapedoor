SPLASHDOWN
Houston · Friday 17 April 1970

What you now know
  1. Mission time counts from lift-off. Add it to the time of lift-off, and you have the time on the wall.
  2. A fuel cell joins hydrogen and oxygen, and gives electricity and water. When Odyssey lost her oxygen, she lost her electricity and her water as well.
  3. So the crew lived in Aquarius, the lunar module, as a lifeboat. They kept Odyssey’s small batteries for the end, because only Odyssey has a heat shield.
  4. Amp-hours divided by amps gives hours. By switching nearly everything off, they made batteries built for two days last almost twice as long.
  5. The danger in the air was not too little oxygen. It was too much carbon dioxide, and lithium hydroxide takes that out.
  6. Air takes the easiest way. A filter needs one way in, one way out, and no gaps.
  7. A procedure is only as good as its words. Nobody at the other end should have to guess.
Nobody in this box knew why the oxygen tank had failed. A board of inquiry answered that two months later. ·
