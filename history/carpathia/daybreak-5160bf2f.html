RECEIVED
The North Atlantic · Monday 15 April 1912

What you now know
  1. The Morse code is dots and dashes. A dash is as long as three dots, and the gaps matter as much as the marks.
  2. In a call, the station that signs after DE is the one that is sending. CQD was the Marconi distress call. SOS, sent as one long sign, was the newer call that the nations had agreed on.
  3. A position is a latitude and a longitude, in degrees and minutes. There are sixty minutes in a degree, not a hundred.
  4. A course is read off the compass rose, at the end of the rule that points where you are going. Distance is measured at the side of the chart, because one minute of latitude is one nautical mile.
  5. A knot is one nautical mile an hour, and time is distance divided by speed. Fifty-eight miles at 17 knots is 3 hours 25 minutes.
  6. A steamship is one kettle. The steam that does not warm the cabins can drive the engines.
  7. Getting ready is most of a rescue, and it has an order: the door is opened before the ladder is hung on it.
