What you now know
  1. A story that reaches you has been copied. Follow it back, copy by copy, to the first one, and ask each sender: where did you get it?
  2. A copy can lose things (a crop cuts off the date, the name, the “says who”) and gain things (somebody writes “official” on top). It never shows more than the copy it was made from.
  3. Six copies of one message are one source, not six.
  4. A fact can be checked, and has been. An opinion cannot be checked at all. And some things could be checked but have not been yet: they are not facts until somebody looks.
  5. Ask who, what, when, where, why and how. A question with no real answer is a hole. Do not fill it with a guess: go and find out.
  6. A headline says only what is known, keeps to the speaker’s own meaning, and says who said it.
  7. Old is not the same as fake. And when you have got something wrong, say so.
