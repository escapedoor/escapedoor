OFF THE NIGHT DESK 08.05

What you now know
  1. Ask of every report: who says so, and how do they know? First-hand is somebody who saw it. Second-hand is somebody who was told.
  2. One caller is one source. Two count as two only if they are independent, like two aunts who do not speak: two people who heard it from the same third person are still one.
  3. Trust people for what they are in a position to know: the agency for the river, the police for the bridge they have shut, a reporter for what she is looking at.
  4. Run what is confirmed. Hold what may be true, and say who can check it. Kill what you know is wrong, or what would put people in danger. Never kill a report because it sounds odd, or because of who sent it.
  5. A photograph can be real and still be old, or from somewhere else. Set what is in it against today: the place, the light, the trees, the signs, any number. One thing that cannot be today is enough.
  6. A name is not a place until you can find it on the map. Look it up, read it to the end, and if two places fit, ask which one.
  7. On the radio a line is heard once: the place first, short sentences, numbers that a voice can say. Say who says so. And say plainly what is not known yet.
