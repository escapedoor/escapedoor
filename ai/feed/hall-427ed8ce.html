What you now know
  1. A recommendation system predicts what each person will watch, finish, like or share, from what they and people who behave like them did before, and ranks the videos by that guess.
  2. It maximises what it measures. Burble counted finishing and sharing, so an angry video people hated could beat a lovely one they liked. “It shows people what they like” is wrong: it shows them what they will do.
  3. Many apps are paid for by advertising, so they are built to keep people watching. Nobody has to be wicked for that to go wrong.
  4. In a feedback loop, what you watch changes what you are shown, which changes what you watch. Angry posts tend to be shared more, so a small difference can grow week by week until two towns see two different worlds.
  5. In a filter bubble your feed agrees with itself, so it feels as if everybody agrees. To find out what is common, count people, not videos, and look at something nobody ranked.
  6. Apps collect personal data (what you watch and for how long, where you pause, where your phone is, your contacts, the time of day) and infer things from it. In the UK you can ask to see it with a subject access request.
  7. Settings change what is counted, and so they change the feed. A feed shows you what gets watched and shared, not what is true or what most people think.
