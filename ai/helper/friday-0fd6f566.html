What you now know
  1. A language model writes one word at a time. It predicts a likely next word from patterns in the text it was trained on.
  2. Likely is not the same as true. Its sentences are fluent, and it sounds just as sure when it is wrong.
  3. It can join true pieces into something false, or invent a quotation, a date or a book that does not exist: a hallucination.
  4. A system knows only what is in its training data. If most examples are wrong, it learns the wrong answer; labels let it learn the right one. A test only checks what is in the test.
  5. To check a claim: find the source, make sure it exists, ask who wrote it and how they know, look at the date, and look for two good sources that agree.
  6. Ask a better question: no answer in it, the right context to read, “where does that come from?”, and “tell me if you are not sure”.
  7. Trust a helper for small things you can easily check. Check it whenever it matters, whenever it gives no source, and whenever the answer sounds too neat. Not every mistake is the machine’s.
