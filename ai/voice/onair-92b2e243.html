What you now know
  1. A synthetic voice is made by a voice model trained on recordings of one person. It can then say new sentences in that voice. Hours of clear speech make a good copy.
  2. What it learnt from leaves traces: words it heard often come out perfectly and rare words oddly, and it copies breathing and the sound of the room.
  3. The ear can tell that something sounds odd, but not that a recording is real or made. A glitch, a breath or a strange word proves nothing by itself.
  4. In a real recording the background goes on underneath everything; where it suddenly changes, something was joined. Mains hum wanders with the grid, and can even show when a recording was made.
  5. Provenance means who had it first, when it was made, and whether the times fit. A second source must be independent: many forwards of one file are still one source.
  6. A true recording can be cut so that it means the opposite. Find the whole recording and hear its context.
  7. A voice belongs to its owner, and copying it needs consent. Before broadcasting, check, put it to the person and think about harm. Never play it and “let the listeners decide”.
